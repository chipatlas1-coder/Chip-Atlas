6061 and 6082 are heat-treatable aluminium–magnesium–silicon alloys of the 6xxx series. 6082 is the European workhorse: Hydro and Aalco both name it the alloy most commonly used for general machining in plate form. 6061 is the closely related alloy found in American specifications such as AMS-QQ-A-250/11. In T6, solution heat-treated and artificially aged, their minimum proof strength is more than twice that of T4.

## Designations

| | 6061 | 6082 |
|---|---|---|
| EN numeric | EN AW-6061 | EN AW-6082 |
| EN chemical | EN AW-AlMg1SiCu | EN AW-AlSi1MgMn |
| Material number | 3.3211 | 3.2315 |
| Old DIN name | AlMg1SiCu | AlMgSi1 |
| UNS | A96061 | A96082 |
| Old BS | H20 | H30 (HE30 for extrusions) |

Most sources give 3.3211 for 6061; a few give 3.3214. The European standards are EN 573-3 for composition (current edition 2026), EN 755-2 for extrusions (2025), EN 485-2 for sheet and plate (2016+A1:2018) and EN 515 for tempers (2017). The property values on this page are as producers and stockists reproduce them, from earlier editions; whether EN 755-2:2025 changed any of them could not be checked.

The two windows exclude each other. 6061 needs at least 0.15 % copper, where 6082 allows at most 0.10 %; and 6082 needs at least 0.40 % manganese, where 6061 allows at most 0.15 %. No melt can be certified to both, so a 6082 certificate does not cover a drawing that calls for 6061, or the reverse.

## Tempers

The temper code after the alloy says how the material was processed. The digit after T describes the heat treatment; 51 means the material was stretched to relieve the stresses left by quenching. The [7075 page](/materials/7075-aluminium/) shows what those stresses do to a machined part.

T6510 is stretched with no further straightening. Hydro also makes producer tempers: T64, underaged for formability, and T66, a T6 with special process control and higher properties.

### US limits and typical values

The Aluminum Association's limits for extrusions (Aluminum Standards and Data, 2009) are 35 ksi (241 MPa) yield and 38 ksi (262 MPa) tensile for 6061-T6, and 38 ksi (262 MPa) and 45 ksi (310 MPa) for 6082-T6 from 0.200 to 6.000 in. For 6061-T6 sheet, AMS-QQ-A-250/11 asks for 35 ksi yield and 42 ksi (290 MPa) tensile. The MPa values are converted here.

Minimums are what a certificate guarantees; typical values are what the material usually shows. The Aluminum Association's typical values for 6061-T6 are Rp0.2 276 MPa, Rm 310 MPa, 95 HB, shear strength 207 MPa and fatigue strength 96.5 MPa at 5 × 10⁸ cycles (MatWeb). For 6082-T6, Aalco's 2013 data sheet gives 290 MPa and 340 MPa.

Two numbers in this table change how a part is machined:

- **Thermal expansion.** From the coefficients, a 100 mm length of aluminium grows about 0.023 mm for a 10 K rise, twice the 0.011 mm of C45E. A part measured warm off the machine is oversize.
- **Elastic modulus.** At 70 GPa against 210 GPa for C45E, a wall of the same shape deflects three times as much under the same cutting force. Thin walls and long overhangs need lighter cuts or support.

6082 needs a fast quench. Nowak and co-workers write that alloys such as EN AW-6082 require high cooling rates, and Hydro notes that T6 may not always be available because of the alloy's quenching limits. Additions of chromium and manganese raise the quench sensitivity of 6xxx alloys (Assaad).

## Welding

Both alloys weld well by MIG and TIG; Wieland and BIKAR rate them 1 for MIG, 2 for TIG and 3 for gas and resistance welding, on a scale where 1 is very good. 4043 is the usual filler for 6082 (Aalco, BlueScope), with 5356 as the alternative (BlueScope). The price is strength: the weld heat dissolves or coarsens the precipitates next to the weld, and Eurocode 9 (EN 1999-1-1) gives reduced design values for this heat-affected zone (HAZ).

## Anodizing

Both alloys anodize very well for protection and only moderately for decoration: Wieland and BIKAR rate protective anodizing 1 and decorative anodizing 3. Hydro rates 6082 at 2 for decorative anodizing on a 1–5 scale, against 5 for 6060 and 6063, and puts this down to its manganese. 6082 still takes clear, dyed and hard anodic coatings (Hydro).

## Machining

Aluminium is ISO 513 group N. ISCAR's group 22, hardenable wrought aluminium at 100 HB, uses kc1.1 = 800 N/mm² and mc = 0.25; the non-hardenable group 21 at 60 HB uses 700 N/mm². At h = 0.2 mm that gives 800 × 0.2<sup>−0.25</sup> ≈ 1196 N/mm², less than half the value for austenitic stainless steel. Shapes by Hydro puts the cutting force in aluminium at about a third of that in steel. No second tool maker's kc table for these alloys could be found; read [cutting forces](/fundamentals/cutting-forces/) before using the figure.

**Machine them hard, not soft.** Wieland and BIKAR rate both alloys 4 when annealed and 2 when age-hardened (1 = very good). Soft material builds up on the insert, makes long chips and leaves burrs; Shapes by Hydro advises machining at the highest practical temper, and notes that chips break more easily as strength rises. Aalco reports that 6082-T6 makes tight coils of swarf with a chip breaker. Lukasak (Sapa) rates 6082 good in T5 and T6 and 6061 only adequate in T6 and T6511, with chips that are harder to break, and recommends chip breakers or peck drilling for both.

**Built-up edge** is the typical tool problem, worst in soft tempers (Shapes by Hydro). Sandvik Coromant's remedy is a higher cutting speed, to raise the temperature, and sharp edges with a smooth rake face; Lukasak adds a finely ground edge and advises against ceramics.

**Speeds** are high. Wieland gives 400–800 m/min for 6082, without naming the tool. TaeguTec's PCD turning grades run from 300 up to 2000–2500 m/min in aged wrought aluminium, and Shapes by Hydro defines high-speed machining of aluminium as 2500 m/min and upward.

**Coolant.** Tungaloy calls for emulsion when machining aluminium with its PCD cutters; Seco rates its uncoated H15 and H25 and coated F15M milling grades for dry or wet cutting.

## Choosing between them

- **Strength.** 6082-T6 is the stronger in extrusions up to 150 mm and in sheet up to 12.5 mm; in thicker plate the minimum proof strength is the same, 240 MPa.
- **Machining.** Aalco rates 6082 good and 6061 acceptable; Hydro and Aalco name 6082 the plate alloy for general machining.
- **Specification.** The compositions exclude each other, so the drawing decides. US documents such as AMS-QQ-A-250/11 specify 6061.
- **Anodizing and welding.** Both weld well and lose about half their proof strength next to the weld; neither is a first choice for decorative anodizing.

Typical uses are similar: structural beams and bridges, cranes, scaffolding, rail and road vehicles, and machined plate parts for 6082 (Hydro, Aalco); shipbuilding, rail coaches, truck frames, military bridges and screw-machine parts for 6061 (Aalco, Hydro).
