C45 is a non-alloy, medium-carbon steel for quenching and tempering: more strength than a structural steel, without alloying elements. It is supplied normalized, soft-annealed, treated for shearability or quenched and tempered.

## Designations

| Name | Number | Note |
|---|---|---|
| C45 | 1.0503 | Base grade |
| C45E | 1.1191 | Lower maximum P and S; formerly **Ck45** (DIN 17200) |
| C45R | 1.1201 | Specified sulphur range 0.020–0.040 %; formerly Cm45 |

Approximate equivalents: AISI/SAE **1045**, JIS **S45C**. Equivalent grades are never identical; check the composition and the properties of the standard you buy to.

The governing standard is **EN ISO 683-1:2018**, which replaced EN 10083-1 and EN 10083-2 (2006). Many mill data sheets still cite EN 10083.

## Chemical composition

Heat analysis, % by mass, EN ISO 683-1:

| C | Si | Mn | P max | S max | Cr max | Mo max | Ni max | Cr+Mo+Ni max | Cu max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.42–0.50 | 0.10–0.40 | 0.50–0.80 | 0.045 (C45) · 0.025 (C45E) | 0.045 (C45) · 0.035 (C45E) | 0.40 | 0.10 | 0.40 | 0.63 | 0.30 |

C45R has S 0.020–0.040 %. The old EN 10083-2 gave silicon as max 0.40 % without a minimum; the 0.10 % minimum comes with EN ISO 683-1. For the maximum P of C45E, Lucefin prints 0.020 %, against 0.025 % in the other sheets.

## Mechanical properties

**Normalized (+N),** minimum values:

| Diameter d (mm) | ReH (MPa) | Rm (MPa) | A (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 16 | 340 | 620 | 14 |
| 16 < d ≤ 100 | 305 | 580 | 16 |
| 100 < d ≤ 250 | 275 | 560 | 16 |

**Quenched and tempered (+QT):**

| Diameter d (mm) | Re min (MPa) | Rm (MPa) | A min (%) | Z min (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 16 | 490 | 700–850 | 14 | 35 |
| 16 < d ≤ 40 | 430 | 650–800 | 16 | 40 |
| 40 < d ≤ 100 | 370 | 630–780 | 17 | 45 |

For C45E and C45R, Saarstahl, Deutsche Edelstahlwerke and thyssenkrupp give a minimum impact energy KV of 25 J in all three bands; Lucefin prints 15 J. The diameter is that of the heat-treated section, and strength falls as the bar gets thicker because C45 hardens only to a limited depth. Its hardenability band for C45E (thyssenkrupp, Lucefin) shows how fast: 55–62 HRC at 1.5 mm from the quenched end, 28–57 HRC at 5 mm, 22–32 HRC at 11 mm.

**Hardness:** soft-annealed (+A) max **207 HB**; treated for shearability (+S) max **255 HB**.

## Heat treatment

Temperatures in °C, as given by the steel makers:

| Treatment | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Normalizing | 840–880 | DEW and Ovako give 840–870 |
| Soft annealing | 650–710 | Saarstahl 680–710; DEW and Ovako 650–700 |
| Hardening | 820–860 | Water or oil (Saarstahl). Lower in water than in oil: DEW 820–850 in water, 830–860 in oil; Ovako 820–850 and 840–870. thyssenkrupp gives 840–880 |
| Tempering | 550–660 | For the +QT strengths above |

## Weldability

Deutsche Edelstahlwerke describes C45E and C45R as difficult to weld and advises against using them in welded structures. Lucefin, if welding cannot be avoided, gives a preheat of 250 °C and stress relieving at 550 °C after welding.

## Machining

C45 is a P-group material in ISO 513. For cutting-force calculations, ISCAR places annealed C45 in its group 2 (190 HB, kc1.1 = 1500 N/mm², mc = 0.22) and Seco uses C45E as the reference material of its group P4 (kc1.1 = 2000 N/mm², mc = 0.15). The two sets differ; read [cutting forces](/fundamentals/cutting-forces/) before mixing them. Ovako offers a calcium-treated "M-Steel" version intended to reduce tool wear and improve chip control.
