"Ra 1.6" is the most common surface specification on a machining drawing, and one of the most misunderstood. A single number cannot describe a surface; each parameter answers one specific question about the profile. Knowing which question it answers is what makes the number useful.

## The profile and the mean line

A stylus instrument traces the surface along a line and records the heights. After the form is removed, a filter with the **cut-off wavelength λc** separates the short-wavelength **roughness** from the longer-wavelength waviness. All R-parameters are calculated from this roughness profile, measured from its **mean line**.

Under ISO 4287 and ISO 4288 the profile is evaluated over **sampling lengths** lr equal to λc; ISO 4288 sets the default **evaluation length** at five sampling lengths.

## Height parameters

**Ra**, the arithmetic mean deviation, is the mean of the absolute heights Z(x) from the mean line:

$$
Ra = \frac{1}{l}\int_0^{l} |Z(x)|\,dx
$$

**Rq** is the root mean square of the same heights. It weights large deviations more strongly than Ra.

**Rp** and **Rv** are the height of the highest peak and the depth of the deepest valley within a sampling length.

**Rz**, the maximum height of the profile, is Rp + Rv within one sampling length. The value reported is the mean of the five values Rz<sub>i</sub> over the five sampling lengths; the largest of them is **Rz1max**.

$$
Rz = \frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5} Rz_i \qquad Rz_i = Rp_i + Rv_i
$$

**Rt**, the total height of the profile, is the highest peak plus the deepest valley over the whole evaluation length. It is therefore always at least as large as Rz.

Ra and Rq average everything, so a single scratch or burr barely changes them. Rz responds more to individual peaks and valleys, and Rt is set by the single worst feature. There is no fixed conversion between Ra and Rz: the ratio depends on the shape of the profile. Sunnen, for example, gives Rz ≈ 7.6 · Ra for surfaces honed with a single grit, and states that its factors do not apply to composite-grit finishes.

<Note kind="warn" title="Rz has meant three different things">
- In ISO 4287:1984, Rz was the **ten-point height**: the mean of the five highest peaks plus the mean of the five deepest valleys. It was deleted as an ISO parameter in 1997; it survives as RzJIS.
- The 1997 ISO Rz is the **maximum height** defined above, which was called Ry before.
- **Rmax** in DIN 4768, VDA and ASME B46.1 means the largest single Rz<sub>i</sub>, the same as Rz1max; in older Japanese standards it meant the total height.

When a drawing or report gives "Rz" without a standard, ask which one.
</Note>

## Shape, spacing and material ratio

- **Rsk** (skewness) is the mean of Z³ divided by Rq³. It is negative when deep valleys interrupt a flat top surface, and positive when isolated peaks stand above it. Two surfaces with the same Ra can have opposite Rsk.
- **Rku** (kurtosis) is the mean of Z⁴ divided by Rq⁴. It equals 3 for a Gaussian profile; higher values mean sharper peaks or valleys.
- **RSm** is the mean width of the profile elements, the spacing of the texture along the surface. In turning it reflects the feed.
- **Rmr(c)**, the material ratio, is the share of the evaluation length in which the profile is cut by a line at height c, in percent. Its curve over all heights, the Abbott–Firestone curve, is the basis of the [Rk parameters](/metrology/rk-parameters/).

## Choosing the cut-off (ISO 4288)

The cut-off λc must suit the roughness being measured. ISO 4288:1996 selected it from the expected value of the parameter:

| Ra (µm) | Rz, Rz1max (µm) | RSm (mm), periodic profiles | λc = lr (mm) | Evaluation length ln (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| over 0.006 to 0.02 | over 0.025 to 0.1 | over 0.013 to 0.04 | 0.08 | 0.4 |
| over 0.02 to 0.1 | over 0.1 to 0.5 | over 0.04 to 0.13 | 0.25 | 1.25 |
| over 0.1 to 2 | over 0.5 to 10 | over 0.13 to 0.4 | 0.8 | 4 |
| over 2 to 10 | over 10 to 50 | over 0.4 to 1.3 | 2.5 | 12.5 |
| over 10 to 80 | over 50 to 200 | over 1.3 to 4 | 8 | 40 |

ISO 3274 pairs each cut-off with a short-wave filter λs and a maximum stylus tip radius: 2.5 µm and 2 µm for λc up to 0.8 mm, 8 µm and 5 µm for 2.5 mm, and 25 µm and 10 µm for 8 mm.

Under ISO 4288 a specification such as Ra 1.6 was met when no more than 16 % of the measured values exceeded it (the 16 % rule). "Ra max 1.6" required every value to stay within it.

## What changed with ISO 21920

ISO 21920-1, -2 and -3, published on 20 December 2021, replaced ISO 1302 (drawing indication), ISO 4287 (parameters) and ISO 4288 (measurement rules); ISO 21920-2 also replaced ISO 13565-2 and -3.

<Figure caption="Three parts of ISO 21920 in place of five earlier standards.">
  <Iso21920Map locale="en" />
</Figure>

The main changes:

| Topic | ISO 4287, 4288, 13565-2 | ISO 21920 |
|---|---|---|
| Sampling length | lr | section length l<sub>sc</sub>, number of sections n<sub>sc</sub> (default 5) |
| Filters | λc, λs | nesting indices N<sub>ic</sub>, N<sub>is</sub> |
| Ra, Rq, Rsk, Rku | averaged over sampling lengths | calculated once over the evaluation length l<sub>e</sub> |
| Rz | mean of five Rz<sub>i</sub> | still the mean over the sections |
| Largest single peak-to-valley height | Rz1max (Rmax in DIN, VDA) | Rzx |
| Material ratios of the core | Mr1, Mr2 (ISO 13565-2) | Rmrk1, Rmrk2 |
| Default acceptance rule | 16 % rule | max rule; the 16 % rule applies only when stated |
| Choice of cut-off | from the measured or expected value | from the tolerance value on the drawing, in setting classes Sc1 to Sc5 |

The Ra bands for choosing the cut-off stayed the same. The Rz bands moved: an upper limit of Rz up to 0.16, 0.8, 16 or 80 µm now selects N<sub>ic</sub> = 0.08, 0.25, 0.8 or 2.5 mm, and above 80 µm, 8 mm.

<Note kind="key" title="Practical consequence">
The same part measured with the same instrument can pass under ISO 4288 and fail under ISO 21920, because the default rule changed from 16 % to max. Drawings should state which standard applies.
</Note>
